Mechanism-level science

We solve the materials problems others can’t explain

Most mineral projects fail because the material is never understood at the level that actually controls performance. We work at that level — where mineralogy, reaction chemistry, interfaces, and processing windows determine whether a feedstock becomes a product or a dead end.

This is not routine testing. This is mechanism-level understanding applied to real materials, commercial constraints, and decision-critical development pathways.

Why clients bring us in

High-value technical clarity before pilot-stage mistakes

We are most useful when a material is difficult, variable, strategically important, or too expensive to keep developing by trial and error.

  • A feedstock looks promising on chemistry but underperforms in practice.
  • Lab results cannot be reproduced or scaled with confidence.
  • Processing costs are rising without clear technical justification.
  • Pilot decisions are approaching while key mechanisms remain unresolved.

The core thesis

Materials do not fail randomly. They fail because reaction pathways, phase constraints, interface behaviour, or processing thresholds are not properly understood. Once those mechanisms are resolved, the development path becomes far clearer.

Every mineral feedstock has a reaction pathway, a processing threshold, and a performance ceiling. Most projects never identify them clearly enough. We do — and then use that understanding to define the most realistic pathway forward.

Hard truth

If you understand the mechanism, you control the outcome. If you don’t, you are guessing.

What we actually solve

This work is built around commercial materials problems, not laboratory theatre. We focus on the technical unknowns that decide whether a mineral stream is worth developing and what it will take to make it perform.

01

Identify what is truly reactive — and what is dead weight

Bulk chemistry alone is not enough. We determine which phases actually participate, which remain inert, and which hidden constraints are likely to dominate behaviour.

What this resolves
  • Reactive vs inert phase contribution
  • Amorphous vs crystalline significance
  • Carbonates, Fe phases, clays, and impurity constraints
  • False positives from chemistry-only screening
02

Map the reaction pathway — not just the starting composition

Performance depends on what forms, how quickly it forms, and whether the system stabilises or passivates. We resolve the sequence, not just the ingredients.

What this resolves
  • Dissolution and ion release behaviour
  • Gel formation and phase evolution pathways
  • Rate-limiting steps and passivation risks
  • Instability masked by early-age results
03

Understand bonding at the scale that controls strength

Strength is not simply a bulk number. It is usually an interface problem. We examine how bonds form, densify, fail, and govern the integrity of the whole system.

What this resolves
  • Interface-controlled bonding mechanisms
  • Gel bridge formation and fracture behaviour
  • Nanoscale mechanics linked to macro performance
  • Weak zones that cap achievable strength
04

Define the minimum viable processing pathway

Over-processing destroys project economics. Under-processing destroys performance. We determine what actually needs to be done — and what can be removed.

What this resolves
  • Grinding, calcination, classification, or chemical activation need
  • Energy–reactivity trade-offs
  • Activation windows and operating thresholds
  • Commercially realistic processing intensity
05

Predict durability before it becomes an expensive problem

Many materials pass early tests and then fail in service or at scale. We evaluate the mechanisms that drive long-term stability, chemical risk, and degradation.

What this resolves
  • Chemical durability and phase stability
  • Shrinkage, cracking, and volume change risks
  • Environmental exposure sensitivity
  • Long-term performance ceilings
06

Explain why a scale-up path is failing

When a system works in a controlled study but breaks under real variability, the issue is rarely “bad luck”. We identify what changed and why the mechanism no longer holds.

What this resolves
  • Reproducibility gaps between lab and pilot
  • Feedstock variability and process sensitivity
  • Humidity, temperature, and mixing boundary effects
  • Hidden failure modes before broader deployment

How we interrogate materials

We do not run techniques for the sake of instrumentation. We select methods based on the mechanism that needs to be resolved and the decision that depends on it.

Phase & structure

Where the material’s mineral logic becomes visible.

  • XRD and quantitative phase analysis
  • Rietveld refinement for complex multi-phase systems
  • FTIR and Raman spectroscopy
  • TGA/DSC for thermal evolution and transformation windows

Interfaces & microstructure

Where bonding, weak zones, and failure pathways are actually controlled.

  • SEM/EDS for morphology and elemental mapping
  • EPMA for higher-resolution compositional interrogation
  • AFM for nanomechanics and surface mapping
  • Fracture surface analysis and intergranular bridge assessment

Chemistry & reaction

Where dissolution, passivation, and phase-forming chemistry are resolved.

  • ICP-based leaching and dissolution kinetics
  • XPS for surface chemistry evolution
  • Solution and solid-state NMR where required
  • Targeted wet chemistry and reaction-state validation
We select methods based on the mechanism we need to resolve — not the equipment we happen to have.

  • Lab performance cannot be reproduced at pilot or demonstration scale.

  • Project teams have data, but not a mechanism-level explanation.

If your material is not simple, this is where projects succeed or fail.
