Projects

Projects that turn mineral uncertainty into development direction

Examples of the kinds of technical-commercial questions we help clarify: whether a feedstock is worth developing, what the main constraints are, and what the most realistic pathway looks like.

Project pattern

Across different feedstocks and applications, the recurring value is the same: better technical decisions earlier, with less wasted effort on unrealistic pathways.

Treated soils for low-cement construction materials

Large volumes of treated and excavated soils are generated with limited high-value reuse options. The key issue is whether these materials can be upgraded into useful low-carbon construction inputs or whether technical limitations make them unsuitable.

Fe-rich slags for alkali-activated binders

Fe-rich slags are often treated as difficult or inconsistent feedstocks. The question is whether they can support stable binder performance and acceptable environmental behaviour.

Carbonation–hydration pathways for secondary mineral resources

Many secondary mineral resources are abundant but difficult to use in conventional cementitious systems. Processing can unlock value, but only within specific windows.

Excavated soils for fired brick applications

Suitability for ceramic products depends on mineral composition, contaminant behaviour, and firing response. Some soil streams are product candidates; others are not.

Calcined clay reactivity and feedstock selection

Calcined clays attract strong market interest, but feedstocks are not interchangeable. Clay mineral type, disorder, and thermal response strongly influence performance.

Industry-linked screening studies

Shorter scoping projects help decide whether pilot work, deeper science, or broader commercial evaluation is justified at all.

What these projects show

Clarify worth

Not every mineral stream deserves pilot work. Early screening separates real opportunity from technical dead ends.

Define route

Projects should narrow whether the pathway is calcination, blending, activation, carbonation, ceramics, or selective civil use.

Reveal constraints

Mineralogy, impurities, variability, and processing demand often decide viability long before formal pilot trials begin.

Turn data into action

The goal is not just characterisation. It is a clearer go / no-go view and a more defensible next step.
