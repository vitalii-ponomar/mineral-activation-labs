Treated soils for low-cement construction materials

Australia
Feedstock reuse decision

The challenge

Large volumes of treated and excavated soils are generated with limited high-value reuse options. The key issue is whether these materials can be upgraded into useful low-carbon construction inputs or whether technical limitations make them unsuitable.

What we did

Assessed mineralogy, chemistry, impurity constraints, and reactivity, then examined realistic processing options including calcination, blending, and alternative binder use.

What became clear

Some soil streams show real potential as reactive binder components, while others are constrained by low clay reactivity, quartz dilution, or contaminant-related risks.

Why it matters

Helps distinguish between materials worth developing and materials likely to consume budget without a credible pathway to product use.

Fe-rich slags for alkali-activated binders

Finland · industry-linked
Slag valorisation

The challenge

Fe-rich slags are often treated as difficult or inconsistent feedstocks. For industrial operators, the core question is whether they can support stable binder performance and acceptable environmental behaviour.

What we did

Examined phase assemblage, activator response, microstructure development, and leaching behaviour to determine how these slag systems perform under practical alkali-activation conditions.

What became clear

Fe-rich slags are not simply problematic by-products. Under the right chemistry, they can function as viable binder precursors, but performance depends strongly on feedstock chemistry, activator design, and process control.

Why it matters

Supports more confident decisions around slag development, product positioning, and when further pilot work is commercially justified.

Carbonation–hydration pathways for secondary mineral resources

Europe · multi-partner research and scale-up context
Processing pathway design

The challenge

Many secondary mineral resources are abundant but difficult to use in conventional cementitious systems. The commercial question is whether processing can unlock enough value to justify further development.

What we did

Developed and evaluated carbonation–hydration routes while tracking phase evolution, microstructure, and resulting material performance under controlled processing conditions.

What became clear

Combined processing can improve phase stability and create more useful binding behaviour, but only within specific processing windows. Not every residue is a strong candidate.

Why it matters

Turns broad waste reuse ambition into a clearer go / no-go view on processing-led product development.

Excavated soils for fired brick applications

Australia
Alternative product route

The challenge

Excavated soils are widely available, but their suitability for ceramic products depends on mineral composition, contaminant behaviour, and firing response. The issue is whether they can become reliable products rather than disposal liabilities.

What we did

Evaluated chemistry, mineralogy, firing behaviour, and material evolution during thermal treatment, with attention to technical and environmental constraints.

What became clear

Some soil streams can be directed toward ceramic products through controlled formulation and firing, while others carry composition or emissions risks that limit practicality.

Why it matters

Provides a clearer basis for deciding whether brick or ceramic product development is realistic for a given soil stream.

Calcined clay reactivity and feedstock selection

Australia · synchrotron-led characterisation context
SCM feedstock qualification

The challenge

Calcined clays are attracting strong market interest, but not all clay-rich materials behave the same way. A major development risk is assuming feedstocks are interchangeable.

What we did

Combined advanced and laboratory characterisation to examine how structural changes during calcination affect reactivity and downstream performance.

What became clear

Feedstock selection matters. Clay mineral type, structural disorder, and thermal response all influence whether a material is likely to perform as a useful SCM or activated precursor.

Why it matters

Supports more disciplined clay selection before larger processing, product-development, or pilot-stage commitments are made.
