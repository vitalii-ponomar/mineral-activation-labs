Soils

Treated soils for low-cement concrete

Australia · reuse pathways
Concrete · soils
Excavated soil feedstock
Coarse-dominated grading
Limited reactive phases
Packing-driven densification
Low-cement concrete performance
Civil reuse application

What we found

Treated soils do not behave as strongly reactive binder replacements. Performance is driven mainly by filler packing, surface nucleation, and matrix densification rather than meaningful pozzolanic contribution.

Where they fit best

Aggregate replacement, flowable fill, and low-cement blends at moderate replacement levels. These routes use the material for grading, packing, and workability rather than overclaiming chemical reactivity.

Commercial value

The project helps avoid expensive overdevelopment of weak SCM candidates while still identifying credible reuse routes with lower technical risk and simpler specification pathways.

Typical outputs

Replacement windows, mix design direction, pathway ranking, and a clear go / no-go position on whether a soil stream should enter concrete trials, granular reuse, or alternative products.

Slags

Fe-rich slags as high-performance binders

What we found

Fe-rich slags can form Fe-bearing calcium silicate hydrate networks with very high early strength. Iron is not just a contaminant or diluent; it participates directly in the binding structure.

Where they fit best

Rapid-strength binders, hybrid cementitious systems, and formulations where activator demand, cost, and leaching behaviour can be tuned together rather than treated separately.

Commercial value

This creates a stronger basis for product positioning of non-traditional slags, especially where performance speed and alternative chemistry offer an advantage over conventional binder thinking.

Typical outputs

Activator windows, strength-performance maps, environmental screening, and decisions on whether a given slag should move toward direct binder use, hybrid systems, or be deprioritised.

What we found

Low-reactivity steel slags can be turned into useful binders under practical CO₂ curing conditions, developing structural strength through coupled carbonation, hydration, and gel reorganisation.

Where they fit best

Precast products, carbonation-bonded mineral systems, and routes where CO₂ uptake and product formation happen in the same process step rather than as a separate environmental add-on.

Commercial value

The work identifies a credible route for residues that are weak in conventional cement systems but stronger when product design is centred on controlled CO₂ curing and early densification.

Typical outputs

CO₂-curing windows, likely product classes, strength-versus-uptake maps, and an early decision on whether a residue belongs in carbonated blocks, hybrid systems, or another pathway entirely.

What we found

Clay performance depends on mineral type, structural disorder, and calcination window rather than temperature alone. Some materials respond strongly; others remain weak even after treatment.

Where they fit best

Kaolinite-rich materials for stronger cement replacement routes, with lower-reactivity clays redirected toward filler roles, ceramics, or activated systems instead of weak SCM positioning.

Commercial value

This improves clay selection before major calcination or pilot commitments and helps distinguish between deposits worth pursuing and materials that should be routed elsewhere.

Typical outputs

Feedstock qualification, calcination windows, likely replacement performance, and a practical route map linking mineralogy to processing demand and product direction.

What we found

Excavated soils can be converted into useful fired products, but viability depends on mineral composition, gas release, contaminant behaviour, and the firing window that controls sintering and porosity.

Where they fit best

Fired bricks, lightweight ceramic products, and encapsulation routes where difficult soils can be stabilised through controlled thermal treatment and product-specific formulation.

Commercial value

The project helps separate soils with real ceramic potential from materials that carry excessive emissions, composition variability, or firing risk and should not be taken into scale-up.

Typical outputs

Firing windows, formulation guidance, risk screening for contaminants and off-gases, and a clearer decision on whether brick or ceramic development is technically justified.

What we found

Sodium silicate can create rigid ceramic-like products through gel bonding and polycondensation, without conventional high-temperature firing or full sintering.

Where they fit best

Architectural tiles, panels, and low-energy mineral products where visual quality, shaping flexibility, and lower thermal demand matter commercially.

Commercial value

This route opens a different product class for waste sand: less focused on bulk construction filler use and more on shaped, higher-value mineral products with lower process energy.

Typical outputs

Curing windows, binder-content direction, product concepts, and an early view on where low-energy mineral products can compete with conventional fired alternatives.

Durability

Long-term durability of alkali-activated slag concrete

Field evidence · 60-year exposure
Durability · AAS

On-site quality control inspection of concrete units
Railway transportation of precast concrete elements
Freshly cut concrete section for microstructural analysis
Outdoor storage of precast concrete structures
Irrigation canal constructed from precast concrete elements

What we found

Natural carbonation in alkali-activated slag concrete is slow, chemically buffered, and structurally heterogeneous rather than a sharp destructive front moving uniformly through the section.

Where it matters

Durability positioning, chemistry-based specification, and realistic service-life assessment for low-carbon slag binders in infrastructure exposed to long-term weathering.

Commercial value

The work gives a stronger evidence base for clients or partners who need confidence that accelerated tests are not over-penalising viable binder systems intended for real structures.

Typical outputs

Durability interpretation, chemistry-linked performance narratives, and a more credible basis for field-oriented specification or product positioning of alkali-activated slag concrete.

Beneficiation

Iron recovery and silica products from tailings

Mineral processing · dual-product recovery
Iron · silica

Fine-grained tailings deposit and slope formation
Magnetising roasting and magnetic separation workflow
Magnetic separation patents

What we found

Fine iron tailings can be magnetically upgraded after controlled roasting, splitting one waste stream into a high-grade iron concentrate and a quartz-rich fine powder.

Where they fit best

Upstream beneficiation where a residue should first be separated into product-grade mineral streams before downstream material development or low-carbon product design begins.

Commercial value

This creates a clearer recovery story, reduces tailings burden, and opens the possibility of linking recovered iron and silica streams into separate industrial markets rather than one compromised reuse case.

Typical outputs

Feed zoning, target fraction selection, beneficiation windows, recovery-grade expectations, and a clearer decision on whether a tailings stream should be processed before any product pathway is assessed.
