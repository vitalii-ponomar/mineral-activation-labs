Applications

Core application categories

Mineral side streams can support a range of construction and mineral-based products. The most suitable pathway depends on mineralogy, chemistry, reactivity, processing demand, and performance requirements.

Supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs)

Selected mineral resources can partially replace Portland cement where chemistry, amorphous content, and fineness support pozzolanic or latent hydraulic behaviour.

Typical candidates

Calcined clay-rich soils, certain metallurgical slags, reactive quarry fines, and aluminosilicate residues.

Alkali-activated and alternative binders

Some mineral streams are better suited to activated binder systems than to direct cement replacement. Suitability depends on dissolution behaviour, phase assemblage, and activator compatibility.

Typical candidates

Slags, Fe-rich industrial byproducts, calcined clays, and blended mineral residues.

Low-cement construction materials

Mineral resources can be incorporated into low-cement formulations where the goal is to reduce clinker demand while maintaining practical performance.

Typical applications

Concrete and mortar blends, flowable fill, controlled low-strength materials, and stabilised construction products.

Stabilisation and engineered fill

Some mineral streams are better suited to large-volume civil applications than to high-reactivity binder systems, particularly where scale and logistics matter.

Typical applications

Soil stabilisation, road base improvement, embankment materials, and engineered fill.

Fired mineral products

Certain clay-rich or mineral-rich materials may be more suitable for thermally processed products than for direct cementitious use.

Typical applications

Bricks, ceramic products, lightweight aggregates, and thermally stabilised materials.

Carbonation-related mineral products

Some calcium- or magnesium-bearing materials can participate in carbonation pathways, enabling carbonated mineral products and CO₂-reactive construction materials.

Typical candidates

Ca- or Mg-rich slags, alkaline residues, and reactive silicate-rich byproducts.

How application pathways are identified

Selecting the right application pathway requires linking material properties with realistic processing and performance targets.

01

Mineralogy

Phase assemblage controls reactivity, phase evolution, and compatibility with binder or stabilisation systems.

02

Chemistry

Bulk and surface chemistry influence binder formation, impurity risks, durability performance, and environmental fit.

03

Processing demand

Grinding, calcination, classification, washing, or activation steps often determine whether a pathway is realistic.

04

Deployment context

Volume availability, supply consistency, location, and logistics strongly influence whether a resource can support a practical product pathway.

Not every feedstock belongs in the same product

Many mineral resources are assessed mainly through the lens of cement replacement. In practice, this is often not the strongest pathway.

Some materials show limited pozzolanic performance but work well in activated systems. Others may not justify complex processing but are well suited to stabilisation or large-volume civil use.

Identifying the right pathway early helps avoid unnecessary testing and keeps development focused on technically realistic opportunities.

Example development pathways

Clay-rich soils

Calcination screening → SCM or low-cement blend potential

Fe-rich slags

Activation assessment → Alternative binder systems

Mixed mineral fines

Processing evaluation → Stabilised fill or civil materials

Calcium-rich residues

Carbonation testing → Carbonated mineral products
