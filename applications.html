Core application categories

Mineral side streams do not belong in a single product pathway. The key is identifying where a material has the strongest technical and commercial fit — and which standards it can realistically meet.

⚗️

Supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs)

SCM pathways are relevant where a mineral resource can deliver cement replacement value without excessive processing or unacceptable performance trade-offs.

What usually controls fit

Reactive phase content, fineness, impurity profile, and compatibility with cement systems.

Standards alignment
  • AS/NZS 3582 series – fly ash, slag, silica, pozzolans
  • AS 3972 – cement compatibility and blending limits
  • ASTM C618 / C1709 – pozzolanic materials and emerging SCMs
Calcined clays Selected slags Reactive fines Mineral residues
🔬

Alkali-activated and alternative binders

Activated binder systems become attractive when direct SCM use is weak, but the feedstock shows stronger response under alkaline conditions.

What usually controls fit

Dissolution behaviour, gel-forming potential, phase assemblage, activator compatibility, and binder stability.

Standards alignment
  • ASTM C1948 – alkali-activated cement specification
  • ASTM C1157 – performance-based cement classification
  • Project-specific performance testing where formal standards remain limited
Slags Fe-rich byproducts Calcined clays Blended residues
🧱

Low-cement concrete and mortar systems

Some feedstocks are best used in practical clinker-reduction strategies rather than full binder replacement.

What usually controls fit

Blend performance, water demand, workability, strength retention, and ease of integration.

Standards alignment
  • AS 3972 – blended cement definitions and general compatibility
  • ASTM C595 – blended hydraulic cements
  • ASTM C1157 – performance-based compliance pathways
Concrete blends Mortars Dry mixes Repair products
🌍

Soil stabilisation and ground improvement

Some materials are better directed into stabilisation pathways than into higher-reactivity binder systems.

What usually controls fit

Geotechnical response, constructability, moisture sensitivity, and deployment at scale.

Standards alignment
  • AS 3798 – earthworks and controlled fill
  • AS 1289 series – soil testing methods
  • Project specifications such as Austroads and TfNSW frameworks
Treated soils Ground improvement Civil works Bulk reuse
🛣️

Road base, subbase, and pavement materials

Mineral side streams can support pavement-related applications where grading, durability, and moisture response align with infrastructure needs.

What usually controls fit

Gradation, stabilisation response, durability, water sensitivity, and practical specification fit.

Standards alignment
  • AS 2758 series – aggregates
  • ASTM D2940 – graded base materials
  • Austroads and transport authority pavement specifications
Road base Subbase Pavements Transport projects
🏗️

Engineered fill and embankment materials

Where a material does not justify a higher-value binder route, engineered fill and embankment use may provide a more realistic outlet.

What usually controls fit

Handling behaviour, environmental suitability, moisture response, and logistics.

Standards alignment
  • AS 3798 – engineered fill classification and control
  • AS 1289 – compaction and density control
  • Environmental and waste classification requirements
Engineered fill Embankments Site formation Bulk placement
🔥

Fired mineral products

Thermally processed products may make more sense where heating produces a useful mineral transformation that is difficult to achieve through cementitious routes.

What usually controls fit

Firing response, shrinkage behaviour, product stability, contaminant behaviour, and energy justification.

Standards alignment
  • AS/NZS 4455 / 4456 – masonry units and testing
  • AS 3700 – masonry durability classes
  • ASTM C216 / C652 – brick performance
Bricks Ceramics Tiles Fired products
🪶

Lightweight aggregates and sintered products

Certain feedstocks may be suitable for expanded, sintered, or thermally transformed aggregate products where density reduction or internal porosity adds value.

What usually controls fit

Thermal expansion behaviour, porosity development, product integrity, and firing practicality.

Standards alignment
  • ASTM C330 – structural lightweight aggregates
  • ASTM C331 – masonry lightweight aggregates
  • AS 2758 – aggregate quality requirements
Lightweight aggregate Expanded products Sintered granules Thermal products
🌫️

Carbonation-cured mineral products

Carbonation routes are worth considering where a feedstock contains reactive phases that can support useful CO₂ uptake and product formation.

What usually controls fit

Carbonation kinetics, accessible reactive phases, curing conditions, and product integrity.

Standards alignment
  • ASTM C1905 – carbonation-cured cements
  • ASTM C1910 – CO₂-cured material testing
  • Emerging standards and project-specific qualification pathways
Blocks Carbonated products CO₂ curing Reactive residues
🛡️

Immobilisation and containment matrices

Some mineral streams are better suited to encapsulation, immobilisation, or controlled containment products than to structural material pathways.

What usually controls fit

Chemical stability, contaminant behaviour, matrix performance, and environmental compliance.

Standards alignment
  • ASTM C1308 – accelerated leach testing
  • ANSI/ANS 16.1 – leachability assessment
  • EPA TCLP (Method 1311) – contaminant mobility screening
Encapsulation Containment Stabilised matrices Environmental fit
⚙️

Fillers, fines replacement, and blend components

Not every useful mineral stream needs to function as a reactive binder precursor. Some create value as fillers, packing components, fines replacements, or blend modifiers.

What usually controls fit

Particle size distribution, mineral compatibility, packing contribution, and formulation stability.

Standards alignment
  • AS/NZS 3582 – mineral additions where relevant
  • AS 1141 – particle size and aggregate testing
  • ASTM routes depend on final formulation and end-use
Fillers Fines replacement Blend modifiers Packing components
✨

Niche mineral products

Some feedstocks are best directed into smaller but more technically targeted pathways where chemistry, colour, texture, or environmental performance create a stronger use case.

What usually controls fit

Distinct mineral properties, processing requirements, product differentiation, and market realism.

Standards alignment
  • Application-specific standards for ceramics, fillers, pigments, or specialty products
  • ISO, ASTM, or client specifications depending on market entry route
  • Performance-driven validation where fixed standards are limited
Decorative products Specialty minerals Targeted formulations Premium niches

Application fit defines value

The same material can fail in one pathway and succeed in another. Most development risk comes from choosing the wrong application — not from the material itself.

What typically goes wrong

  • forcing weak SCM candidates into cement replacement roles
  • overestimating reactivity instead of using bulk-value pathways
  • testing broadly without a defined product direction
  • ignoring standards until late-stage development

What works better

  • start from the strongest technically defensible application
  • align early with realistic standards and specifications
  • prioritise pathways that minimise processing complexity
  • treat application selection as the core decision, not an outcome
