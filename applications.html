Application pathways

Different mineral streams create value in different product categories.

The right route depends on mineralogy, chemistry, processing demand, performance needs, and supply reality. A material only becomes attractive when the pathway matches the feedstock.

Pathway discipline matters
  • Not every feedstock should chase cement replacementSome win elsewhere.
  • Application logic decides what to proveStrength, durability, curing, emissions, colour, or processing may become the lead variable.
  • The best route is the one that can scaleNot just the one that looks exciting in a lab result.
Core application categories

Practical routes for underused mineral feedstocks and residues.

The categories below cover the main directions where feedstocks can become meaningful products or components.

SCM route

Supplementary cementitious materials

For feedstocks with useful reactive fractions, suitable chemistry, and a realistic fit within blended cement or concrete systems.

Activation route

Alkali-activated and alternative binders

For slag-rich, aluminosilicate, or specially formulated systems where conventional clinker-heavy routes are not the best use case.

Civil route

Stabilisation, fill, and ground improvement

For materials that may be highly useful in infrastructure even when they are not premium binder feedstocks.

Thermal route

Fired products and ceramic-like materials

For clays, excavated soils, and blended mineral systems where thermal processing can unlock structural or decorative value.

Carbon route

Carbonation-related mineral products

For calcium- and magnesium-bearing systems relevant to mineral curing, carbon uptake, and carbon-linked performance gains.

Hybrid route

Multi-stream formulations

For systems where the opportunity only appears when several feedstocks are combined to balance chemistry, cost, and performance.

Selection logic

The right question is not “what can this material do?” but “where can it win?”

Good application selection reduces wasted trials, improves partner conversations, and makes the technical case much easier to communicate.
