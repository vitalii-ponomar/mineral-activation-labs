From mineral side-streams to construction materials

Unlocking value in mineral waste and industrial side-streams

We help Australian industry identify, qualify, and develop natural and industrial mineral side-streams — including mine tailings, metallurgical slags, clay-rich soils, quarry fines, and other mineral residues — for use in low-carbon construction materials.

Many of these materials are currently landfilled or stockpiled despite containing valuable mineral phases. With the right understanding of mineralogy, reactivity, and processing pathways, they can become useful material resources.

Why this matters now

Australia generates large volumes of mineral side-streams from mining, mineral processing, infrastructure works, and construction activities. Many of these materials remain underutilised because their technical suitability for new applications is unclear.

01

Large underused mineral streams

Tailings, slags, soils, fines, and mineral residues are often stockpiled or landfilled despite containing potentially useful mineral phases.

02

Pressure to cut emissions and landfill

Construction and resource industries face growing pressure to reduce cement use, improve circularity, and find better pathways for by-product reuse.

03

Need for practical qualification

Unlocking value requires more than chemistry alone. It needs mineralogical insight, reaction pathways, and realistic routes into materials applications.

Mineralogical interpretation

Immobilisation and stabilisation

How projects usually start

Most projects begin with a short technical review of the available material data. From there, we identify realistic activation pathways and define the next testing steps.

1

Send feedstock data

2

Initial technical screening

3

Activation pathways

Potential strategies may include calcination, alkaline activation, blending, or stabilisation.

4

Testing or pilot concept

Define the most useful laboratory tests or pilot steps to evaluate the material.
