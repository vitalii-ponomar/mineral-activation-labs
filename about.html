About

Core technical expertise

Mineral Activation Labs brings together expertise across civil engineering, mineralogy, geochemistry, and cementitious materials to evaluate underused mineral resources for low-carbon construction materials and related mineral products.

01

Technical foundation

The platform is built around the technical disciplines most relevant to feedstock qualification: mineralogical interpretation, reaction-driven chemistry, processing realism, and performance relevance in engineered material systems.

  • Construction relevance of mineral resources

Mineralogy and geochemistry

  • Reactivity controls and pathway suitability
  • Dissolution, precipitation, and solution chemistry

Feedstock systems

  • Treated soils and industrial residues
  • Tailings, fines, and mineral side streams

Scientific background

The work behind Mineral Activation Labs is informed by active research linked to UNSW Sydney, including expertise connected to the School of Civil and Environmental Engineering.

02

Research-linked foundation

Its scientific basis includes low-carbon cementitious systems, alkali-activated materials, sodium silicate chemistry, calcined clays, silicate-rich mineral systems, Fe-rich byproducts, and the reactivity of difficult mineral streams considered for construction applications.

Research themes

  • Low-carbon cement and concrete materials
  • Alkali-activated and silicate-based systems
  • Calcined clays and clay-rich soils
  • Fe-rich byproducts and industrial residues

Practical value of this background

  • More rigorous interpretation of uncertain materials
  • Better understanding of pathway fit and process demand
  • Stronger link between mechanism and application
  • Higher-quality early-stage technical decisions

Team

Mineral Activation Labs is supported by a multidisciplinary scientific team with backgrounds across civil engineering, mineralogy, geochemistry, cement and concrete materials, and waste-derived mineral systems.

Multidisciplinary capability

The team combines research depth, laboratory-based materials understanding, and applied interpretation of how unconventional mineral streams behave in engineered systems. This structure allows technical questions to be approached from multiple angles rather than from a single testing or consulting lens.

Materials and binder chemistry

Mineral assessment and reactivity

Expertise in interpreting mineral feedstocks through phase assemblage, transformation behaviour, reaction potential, and likely technical constraints.

Applied technical direction

Team capability focused on turning scientific data into clearer decisions about whether a feedstock should move forward and under what development pathway.
