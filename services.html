Core advisory capabilities

Mineral Activation Labs supports organisations working with mineral resources and industrial side-streams that may have value in low-carbon construction materials. Our work is built around three practical development questions: is the material worth developing, what is technically driving its behaviour, and what pathway could make it viable?

Feedstock intelligence

We assess whether a mineral stream represents a credible technical opportunity. This includes mineral resources and industrial side‑streams such as mine tailings, metallurgical slags, clay‑rich soils, quarry fines, excavation materials, and mineral processing residues.

  • Mineralogical and chemical interpretation
  • Identification of reactive and inert phases
  • Assessment of contaminants, heterogeneity, and supply constraints
  • Early technical feasibility assessment

Outcome: a clear view of whether the material deserves further development.

Reactivity & material potential

We determine whether a feedstock can function in cementitious or mineral binder systems by linking mineral structure and phase assemblage to realistic performance potential.

  • Pozzolanic reactivity
  • Latent hydraulic behaviour
  • Alkali‑activation potential
  • Calcination pathways for clay‑rich materials
  • Relevance for concrete, stabilisation, and low‑carbon binders

Outcome: identification of viable binder pathways.

Development pathways

For promising materials we define the most credible route to unlock value, matching processing intensity with achievable product value.

  • Thermal activation and calcined‑clay pathways
  • Grinding, classification, or beneficiation strategies
  • Washing and impurity‑management options
  • Blending with cementitious materials
  • Stabilisation and ground‑improvement uses

Outcome: a technically realistic development direction.

What each engagement may include

Projects are structured around a specific decision or development question rather than a fixed testing package. Depending on the material and stage of work, an engagement may combine several of the elements below.

Feedstock screening and qualification

  • Review of available chemistry, mineralogy, and process background
  • Assessment of likely reactive phases, inert fractions, and undesirable components
  • Identification of contaminants, heterogeneity, and practical development risks
  • Initial view on suitability for cementitious or related mineral applications
  • Go / no-go or priority ranking across one or multiple materials

Reactivity and application relevance

  • Assessment of pozzolanic, latent hydraulic, or alkali-activation potential
  • Interpretation of which phases are most likely to contribute to performance
  • Consideration of thermal activation potential for clays and fine mineral streams
  • Comparison of candidate pathways for SCM use, activated binders, or blended systems
  • Identification of the main technical unknowns that should be resolved next

Mineralogical interpretation and technical insight

  • Translation of XRD, XRF, and thermal data into development-relevant conclusions
  • Distinction between attractive bulk chemistry and useful reactive mineral content
  • Interpretation of how mineralogy may influence processing demand and variability
  • Explanation of why apparently similar residues can behave very differently
  • Framing of opportunity around realistic technical mechanisms rather than generic claims

Pathway definition and next-step strategy

  • Review of likely needs for grinding, classification, washing, drying, calcination, or blending
  • Assessment of whether thermal or chemical activation is technically justified
  • Matching of process intensity to the probable value of the end application
  • Definition of the most useful next laboratory, validation, or pilot-oriented tests
  • Clear recommendation on what should happen next and why

How projects usually start

Most collaborations begin with a focused feedstock evaluation. The aim is to move quickly from raw material information to a clear development direction.

1

Review the material

We start with the feedstock itself: what it is, where it comes from, what is already known, and what opportunity or constraint the client is trying to assess.

2

Screen the fundamentals

We review mineralogy, chemistry, likely reactivity, contaminants, variability, and probable processing demand to understand whether the material has credible technical potential.

3

Define the pathway

We identify where the strongest technical case may sit, including likely activation routes, target applications, and the main issues that need to be resolved next.

4

Recommend next steps

The output is a practical next decision: targeted testing, deeper evaluation, pilot preparation, reprioritisation, or an early no-go where justified.
