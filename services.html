Core services

Mineral Activation Labs provides specialised technical advisory for organisations working with mineral resources and industrial side‑streams in Australia. The focus is on determining whether materials such as mine tailings, metallurgical slags, clay‑rich soils, quarry fines, and excavation materials can realistically be developed into useful feedstocks for low‑carbon construction materials and related mineral products.

Feedstock qualification

Technical assessment of whether a mineral resource or industrial residue is genuinely worth developing. This stage focuses on mineral quality, variability, contaminants, and alignment with realistic construction-material applications in the Australian market.

Reactivity evaluation

Evaluation of whether a material can perform as a supplementary cementitious material (SCM), alkali‑activated precursor, mineral filler, or stabilisation component in low‑carbon binders used in concrete, infrastructure, and ground improvement applications.

Mineralogical interpretation

Translation of mineralogical, chemical, and thermal data into development‑relevant insight. This helps distinguish materials that merely look promising on bulk chemistry from those with genuinely useful reactive mineral phases.

Activation and processing strategy

Identification of technically realistic processing routes such as calcination of clay soils, grinding and classification of tailings, alkaline activation, blending with cementitious materials, or stabilisation strategies suited to Australian material streams.

Performance‑oriented test planning

Definition of the most informative next testing program for evaluating strength development, binder behaviour, durability indicators, and early product feasibility in construction material systems.

Technical development advice

Integration of the technical findings into a clear development view: what is promising, what constraints exist, and what the most credible pathway forward should be before pilot‑scale investment.

What each service can include

Each project is scoped around a practical technical question. Work may combine feedstock screening, mineralogical interpretation, pathway definition, and targeted development advice depending on the material and the opportunity.

Feedstock qualification and screening

  • Review of available chemistry, mineralogy, and background process data
  • Assessment of reactive phases, inert fractions, and undesirable components
  • Identification of contaminants or variability risks common in tailings and residues
  • Initial judgement on suitability for cementitious or mineral construction uses
  • Priority ranking across multiple materials or deposits

Reactivity and application relevance

  • Assessment of pozzolanic, latent hydraulic, or alkali‑activation potential
  • Identification of phases most likely to contribute to binder performance
  • Consideration of calcination pathways for clay soils or fine mineral streams
  • Evaluation of SCM potential for cement and concrete systems
  • Clarification of technical unknowns requiring further testing

Mineralogical interpretation and technical insight

  • Translation of XRD, XRF, and thermal data into development‑relevant insight
  • Distinction between bulk chemistry and useful reactive mineral content
  • Interpretation of how mineralogy affects processing demand and reactivity
  • Identification of phases that control behaviour in binder systems
  • Explanation of why apparently similar residues behave differently

Processing, activation, and next‑step strategy

  • Evaluation of grinding, classification, washing, or beneficiation needs
  • Assessment of calcination or chemical activation options
  • Matching process intensity to likely product value
  • Definition of the most useful next laboratory or pilot tests
  • Clear recommendation for the next development step

How projects usually start

Most engagements begin with a focused technical review of the feedstock and the decision behind it. The objective is to move quickly from raw material information to a credible development direction.

1

Define the material and the question

We start with the feedstock itself: what it is, where it comes from, what is known about it, and what decision the client is trying to make.

2

Screen the technical fundamentals

We review the material through the lens of mineralogy, chemistry, reactivity, contaminants, variability, and likely processing demand.

3

Identify the most credible pathway

We determine where the strongest technical case may sit, including likely activation routes, end-use logic, and the main constraints that need to be addressed.

4

Recommend the next decision

The output is a clear next step: targeted testing, deeper evaluation, pilot preparation, reprioritisation, or an early no-go where justified.
